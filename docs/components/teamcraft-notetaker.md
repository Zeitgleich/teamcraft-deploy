# TeamCraft notetaker

Optional, off by default. The notetaker is a participant people can ask into a call ("Add notetaker"
in the call's menu); afterwards it posts a summary and the transcript into the call's conversation. It
is a headless TeamCraft client with its own bot account and device, like the
[integration bridge](teamcraft-bridge.md). Rootless as `tc-notetaker`; it **listens on no port**: it
connects out to the server, the SFU (QUIC/UDP) and `https://openrouter.ai`. What it does in detail, and
the settings admins make in the app: `docs/admin.md` §3.7 and `docs/e2ee.md` §8.2 in the TeamCraft
repository.

## Before you turn it on

**Privacy.** The notetaker takes the call's audio **out of the end-to-end encryption**:

- **Whoever controls this host can hear the calls the notetaker is in.** It holds the bot's keys, the
  keys of the calls it joins and the content keys of its conversations; someone who takes over the
  host (or its root account, or `tc-notetaker`) can listen in and read those conversations. Run it
  only on a host you trust as much as the calls.
- **The audio goes to OpenRouter** (a company in the USA) and on to the provider that runs the
  transcription model; the transcript goes to the summary model the same way. Every participant sees a
  banner and a changed encryption badge while it is in the call, and it posts where the audio goes.

**Data processing (GDPR).** The organization is the controller of the call audio and the transcripts;
OpenRouter and the model's provider are processors. Before you use it: conclude a data processing
agreement with OpenRouter, check the providers its routing may use (keep "Zero data retention only"
on), have a transfer mechanism for the USA, tell people before they join (Art. 13 GDPR) and have a legal
basis; recording non-public speech may need everybody's consent (in Germany § 201 StGB), and a works
council usually has a say (§ 87 (1) no. 6 BetrVG). The full notes are in the TeamCraft repository's
`docs/admin.md` §3.7, "Data processing (GDPR)". No audio is kept anywhere; summaries and transcripts are
messages and follow the conversation's retention.

**Cost.** OpenRouter bills the transcription per second of speech and the summary per token: with the
default models (Voxtral Mini Transcribe 3 and Mistral Large) about **$0.22 per meeting hour**
(about $0.21 for an hour of speech plus about $0.01 for its summary). Silence is not sent; overlapping
speakers count once each. The notetaker logs what each call cost (`journalctl --user -u
teamcraft-notetaker` as `tc-notetaker`). Set a credit limit on the OpenRouter key.

## Setup

1. **Admin console → Integrations** (or `POST /api/v1/admin/bots`): create a bot with a name people
   recognize, such as "Notetaker". Its token (`tcb_...`) is shown once.
2. Put it in the sops file ([../configuring.md](../configuring.md#secrets)) as `notetaker_bot_token`.
   It can't be generated; the database key (`notetaker_store_key`) and the recovery passphrase of the
   bot's backup key (`notetaker_passphrase`) are.
3. Turn the role on and deploy once:

   ```yaml
   # inventory/host_vars/chat.example.com/vars.yml
   teamcraft_notetaker_enabled: true
   ```

   `just install-all` (or `just install-component teamcraft-notetaker`). On its first start the
   notetaker enrolls the bot's device and backs up its keys with `notetaker_passphrase`; there is
   nothing to confirm by hand.
4. **Administration → Notetaker** (owners sign it, like every policy page): turn it on, pick the bot
   account, choose who may add it, the models, the language and the summary instructions, and enter the
   OpenRouter API key. The server stores the key encrypted with `settings_key` (generated by the base
   role) and hands it only to the bot; the notetaker refuses a key whose hash isn't the signed one.
5. Add the bot to the channels where it may be used (**Channel settings → Integrations**), or let
   people tick "Notetaker joins the calls" when they schedule a meeting. It can only join calls of
   conversations it is a member of, and must be in before the call starts.

Steps 1, 4 and 5 happen in the app and can't be automated by the playbook.

**The OpenRouter key on this host instead (optional).** To keep the key off the server, put it in the
sops file as `notetaker_openrouter_api_key`: the role passes it to the notetaker
(`openrouter.api_key`), which then doesn't fetch it. Enter the same key in Administration → Notetaker
all the same; the console then signs only its hash.

| Setting                                                  | Default                                            | Meaning                                                      |
|----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| `teamcraft_notetaker_enabled`                            | `false`                                            | run the notetaker                                            |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_image`                              | `ghcr.io/zeitgleich/teamcraft-notetaker:<version>` | image (follows `teamcraft_version`)                          |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_server_url`                         | `https://<teamcraft_domain>`                       | the server the bot signs in to                               |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_device_name`                        | `<teamcraft_domain> notetaker`                     | the bot's device name                                        |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_memory`                             | `1g`                                               | memory limit                                                 |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_log_filter`                         | `info`                                             | `RUST_LOG`                                                   |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_openrouter_base_url`                | `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1`                     | OpenRouter's API                                             |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_openrouter_request_timeout_secs`    | `120`                                              | one request's longest time                                   |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_openrouter_max_retries`             | `4`                                                | retries of rate limits, timeouts and server errors           |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_openrouter_max_concurrent_requests` | `4`                                                | requests at the same time, all calls together                |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_max_call_minutes`                   | `240`                                              | it leaves a call after this long and posts what it has       |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_alone_timeout_secs`                 | `60`                                               | it leaves a call where nobody else has been for this long    |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_shutdown_drain_secs`                | `60`                                               | at a stop or restart, calls in progress get this long        |
| `teamcraft_notetaker_health_max_age_secs`                | `120`                                              | the health check fails when the liveness file is older       |

The models, the language and the summary instructions are not set here: they are the owner-signed
workspace policy, so the server can't change them.

## Image, health and updates

The image is `ghcr.io/zeitgleich/teamcraft-notetaker`, tagged like the server, so it follows
`teamcraft_version`: `just update` and `just install-all` move it with the rest, and a changed image or
configuration restarts it (it first finishes the calls in progress, up to
`teamcraft_notetaker_shutdown_drain_secs`, and posts the rest at its next start). The image is
published from the TeamCraft release that added the notetaker on; with an older `teamcraft_version`
there is no image to pull.

The container's health check is `teamcraft-notetaker healthcheck`: with no port to probe, it reads a
liveness file the worker rewrites every 10 seconds in the volume (`notetaker.alive`). The unit restarts
the container when it exits.

## Data and recovery

The notetaker's database (the bot's keys, its conversations, the transcripts of calls in progress) is
the podman volume `teamcraft-notetaker-data` of `tc-notetaker`, mounted at `/var/lib/teamcraft-notetaker`
and encrypted with `notetaker_store_key`. Call audio never touches the disk. The volume is not in the
backups: when it is lost, the notetaker enrolls a new device and restores the bot's keys from the server
with `notetaker_passphrase`. Keep both secrets (they are in `/srv/teamcraft/secrets` or your sops file).

Disabling the role stops the notetaker and removes its unit; the volume stays
([../uninstalling.md](../uninstalling.md)). Turn the notetaker off in Administration → Notetaker as
well, and when you stop using it for good, delete the volume (it keeps the messages of its
conversations like any device) and revoke the bot in the admin console.
